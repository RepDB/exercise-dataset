Copenhagen Plank

A demanding side plank in which the top leg rests on a flat bench and the lower leg is held off the floor. The inner thigh muscles of the top leg and the obliques hold the body in a straight line, which makes it a classic exercise for the adductors and the trunk.

How to do the Copenhagen Plank

  1. Lie on your side next to a flat bench and place your top leg on it, with the inside of the foot or the lower part of the leg resting on the bench.
  2. Prop yourself up on the elbow of your lower arm, directly under your shoulder.
  3. Lift your hips so that your body forms a straight line from your head to your top foot, and let the lower leg hang or lift it toward the bench.
  4. Keep your hips high and your trunk steady, and breathe evenly.
  5. Hold for the planned time, then lower your hips back to the floor.
  6. Rest, then switch sides.

Form tips

  • Start with the top knee on the bench and a short lever; move the foot of the top leg onto the bench only when the hold feels easy.
  • Press your forearm into the floor and keep your shoulder away from your ear.
  • Stop if you feel a sharp pull in the groin; the adductors need a gentle build-up.
